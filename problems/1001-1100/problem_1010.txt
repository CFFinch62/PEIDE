Problem 1010: March of the Penguins

<p>
$m$ penguins start on the real number line at positions $1,\dots,m$, all facing in the positive direction. At each step, a single penguin marches forward $1$ unit. However, as no two penguins can occupy the same position, a penguin is only able to move if the space ahead of it is currently free.</p>

<p>
For example, for $m=2$ penguins there are six ways their march can proceed over four steps:
$$\begin{align}
&amp;(1,2)\rightarrow(1,3)\rightarrow(1,4)\rightarrow(1,5)\rightarrow(1,6)\\
&amp;(1,2)\rightarrow(1,3)\rightarrow(1,4)\rightarrow(1,5)\rightarrow(2,5)\\
&amp;(1,2)\rightarrow(1,3)\rightarrow(1,4)\rightarrow(2,4)\rightarrow(2,5)\\
&amp;(1,2)\rightarrow(1,3)\rightarrow(1,4)\rightarrow(2,4)\rightarrow(3,4)\\
&amp;(1,2)\rightarrow(1,3)\rightarrow(2,3)\rightarrow(2,4)\rightarrow(2,5)\\
&amp;(1,2)\rightarrow(1,3)\rightarrow(2,3)\rightarrow(2,4)\rightarrow(3,4)
\end{align}
$$
</p>

<p>
We see there is one way the march ends with final position vector $(1,6)$; three ways it ends at $(2,5)$; and two ways it ends at $(3,4)$.</p>

<p>
Given the number of penguins $m$ and the total number of steps $n$, define $f(v)$ to be the number of ways the march ends with the final position vector $v$. From the example above, with $m=2$ and $n=4$:
$$\begin{align}
f((1,6))&amp;=1\\
f((2,5))&amp;=3\\
f((3,4))&amp;=2
\end{align}
$$
Define $F(m, n)$ to be the <b>product</b> of $f(v)$ over all $v$ such that $f(v)$ is nonzero. For example $F(2,4)=1\times3\times2=6$.</p>

<p>
You are also given $F(3,6)=180000$ and $F(5,10)\equiv 411456133\bmod{1234567891}$.</p>

<p>
Find $F(150,300)$ giving your answer modulo $1234567891$.</p>
